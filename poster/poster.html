Before AI, everything is far from everything, even two photos of the same person. Training on faces pulls each person together; the loss decides how, and at what cost.

The 4 losses: what each one asks of the space tuned values

Triplet

L = [ d(a,p) − d(a,n) + m ]+

Each photo must be closer to its own person than to another one, by a margin m = 0.04.

A relative rule: tight groups, people moderately spread.

Contrastive

L = [ d(a,p) − m+ ]+ + [ m− − d(a,n) ]+

Pulls two photos of a person closer than m+ = 0.40; pushes other people beyond m− = 0.65, then stops.

Very tight groups, but people only need to be a little apart: crowding.

Norm. softmax

L = −log es cos θyΣj es cos θj

Each training person has a point on the sphere. A photo is pulled to its own and pushed from all 7,689 others (s = 31).

People spread over the whole sphere.

ArcFace

L = −log es cos(θy + m)es cos(θy + m) + Σj≠y es cos θj

Same, but the photo must win even with an extra angle m = 0.45 rad (≈ 26°), s = 37.

Spread like softmax, with tighter groups.

a: a photo · p: another photo of the same person · n: a photo of someone else · d: distance on the sphere · θj: angle between a photo and person j · [x]+ = max(x, 0). The effects are read from the formulas and agree with the map; they are an interpretation, not a proof.

Take-home

  • Before AI, nothing is pulled together: photos are spread out, but a person's photos are not close.
  • Training on faces pulls each person together: +30 points over the best classical method.
  • The loss shapes the rest: the tightest groups (Contrastive) crowd people together; the best losses balance tight and spread.

Limits: one dataset (CelebA), one network (ResNet-18), validation numbers until the test set is read once.
